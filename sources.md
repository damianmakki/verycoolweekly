# Very Cool Weekly — content sources

Reviewed: **November 3, 2026**.

Current inventory: **102 regular discovery sources**, **26 browser-first watchlist entries**, and **4 entries awaiting recheck** — **132 distinct entry points** in total. “Regular” means the entry page was readable when checked, not that it has a weekly feed or that every linked work has been tested.

A messy, curious collection for creators, designers, and developers. Good design, code, art, games, sound, objects, internet history, niche obsessions, and things that are simply too strange or delightful to leave out. A pick does not need to make someone more productive to belong here. The categories, priorities, and suggested angles below are editorial recommendations. Linked pages were checked during research; that does not mean every site publishes weekly or every article is new.

Use these sites to discover candidates. Where a publication or community points to someone else's work, visit the original creator, project, or documentation before selecting it. Link to that original source in the issue and credit the place we discovered it when useful.

## Start somewhere different each week

Do not work through the same ten design blogs and bolt a novelty link onto the end. Rotate the entry points so the source pool itself stays broad.

| Route | Start with | Follow the thread toward |
| --- | --- | --- |
| The handmade web | [Tiny Awards](https://tinyawards.net/), [the html review](https://thehtml.review/), [Wiby](https://www.wiby.me/) | A personal site, web poem, small game, or beautifully unnecessary experiment. |
| Rabbit holes | [things magazine](https://www.thingsmagazine.net/), [kottke.org](https://kottke.org/), [Are.na](https://www.are.na/) | A maker's own project, an obsessive collection, or a connection between unrelated disciplines. |
| Physical weirdness | [Core77](https://www.core77.com/), [Unnecessary Inventions](https://unnecessaryinventions.com/), [Hackaday](https://hackaday.com/) | Ingenious mechanisms, obsessive prototypes, industrial design, absurd objects, and alternative ways of making things. |
| Art and experiments | [BOOOOOOOM](https://www.booooooom.com/), [OpenProcessing](https://openprocessing.org/), [itch.io](https://itch.io/) | Illustration, creative code, odd games, animation, sound, and the people making them. |
| Useful craft | [Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/), [Smashing Magazine](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/), [Ahmad Shadeed](https://ishadeed.com/) | A specific technique, clear explanation, or useful tool worth putting to work. |
| Sound and signals | [CDM](https://cdm.link/), [NIME](https://nime.org/), [Disquiet](https://disquiet.com/), [NTS](https://www.nts.live/) | Invented instruments, an experimental performance, an unusual listening trail, or a bridge between music and interface design. |
| Strange infrastructure | [Solar Protocol](https://solarprotocol.net/), [BLDGBLOG](https://www.bldgblog.com/), [Ink & Switch](https://www.inkandswitch.com/) | Networks shaped by sunlight, spatial oddities, or a different way of building creative software. |
| Paper and print | [Present & Correct](https://www.presentandcorrect.com/blogs/blog), [Slanted](https://www.slanted.de/), [Spoon & Tamago](https://spoon-tamago.com/) | Ephemera, experimental lettering, overlooked objects, and visual culture outside the same agency portfolios. |
| Past lives of things | [The Public Domain Review](https://publicdomainreview.org/), [Web Design Museum](https://www.webdesignmuseum.org/), [Cameron's World](https://www.cameronsworld.net/) | Forgotten visual languages, old interfaces, strange documents, and rediscovered histories. |
| Material behavior | [Institute of Making](https://www.instituteofmaking.org.uk/), [MaterialDistrict](https://materialdistrict.com/), [AMOLF](https://www.amolf.nl/news/), [Materiom](https://www.materiom.org/) | A curious substance, responsive surface, growing material, or fabrication process; follow the evidence as well as the photograph. |

For a design/music/photo detour, start with ISO50’s archive, Grain Edit, or but does it float, then follow the original maker forward to their current work. Keep the atmosphere and cross-disciplinary curiosity; do not mistake old posts for new releases.

On each research run, explore at least four of these routes. Spend roughly half the discovery effort outside mainstream design/front-end publications. Follow at least two links onward to independent creators or small personal sites. Actively sample at least two of the sound, physical-object, paper/print, and material routes on each run, rotating the starting sites; across successive editions, music, industrial design, graphic design, and materials should recur as real threads. These are collection guidelines, not compulsory slots in the finished edition. A single issue does not need one of everything.

## A repeatable weekly collection routine

Research starts Monday at 2 p.m. Mountain, with a shortlist of 8–12 candidates for review. Review before Tuesday's individual X posts; six approved picks still fit the existing Tuesday–Thursday drip and Friday roundup.

1. Read the saved preference brief, then pick at least four different discovery routes above. Use preferences to guide curiosity, not fence it in.
2. Collect across practical work, visual culture, experimental web, physical making, and stranger side roads. Aim for 3–5 genuinely unexpected candidates in a 10–12-pick shortlist when the material is strong. Don't manufacture weirdness to hit a quota.
3. Follow original makers, try accessible interactions, check dates and claims, and write why each find caught our attention. A beautiful, funny, unsettling, or useless thing can stand on its own without a business or productivity takeaway.
4. Keep recent work alongside honestly labeled older discoveries. Avoid an issue made entirely of launches, agency profiles, or recycled viral links. The finished edit should feel like someone interesting emptied their browser tabs onto the table.
5. Before Friday publication, recheck final links, permissions, disclosures, and all channel credits. Keep current review decisions and notes intact.

There is no fixed two-design/two-development/one-tool/one-wildcard formula. Keep the overall promise to creators, designers, and developers while letting an individual issue be lopsided when the finds deserve it. Aim for at least four distinct original creators or publishers and normally no more than two picks from one publisher. A deep technical piece can sit next to a web poem, an eccentric physical object, and an internet-history rabbit hole.

## Collection methods and automation

- Use the publishers' own RSS links or newsletters where offered. Save a small set of core bookmarks so manual collection still works when feeds change.
- Feed entry points found on the reviewed sites: [The Pudding RSS](https://pudding.cool/rss.xml) and [Piccalilli's feed directory](https://piccalil.li/rss/), which lists separate article, link, newsletter, and topic feeds. These are discovery links, not a tested automated ingestion integration; verify feed responses before adding a collector.
- A future collector should record title, original URL, date, source, and a short note in a **candidate inbox**. It should not copy full articles into issue files or change an issue to published.
- Keep a last-seen timestamp per source and deduplicate by original URL. Recheck redirects and article dates before drafting, rather than assuming a feed entry is a fresh publication.
- Prefer an available feed or documented API to scraping pages. Respect source access restrictions and avoid paywall workarounds.
- Review this list monthly. Drop stale sources, replace broken links, and add original creators we discover through the broader publications.

## Commercial opportunities without compromising the edit

Product Hunt, Show HN, Figma stories, Framer updates, and GitHub projects can reveal products relevant to our audience. Design Milk and Fonts In Use can surface physical products, books, and type foundries. These are **leads**, not verified advertisers or affiliate partners.

Select useful editorial content first. Separately assess audience fit and the creator's official commercial/contact information. An editorial mention does not depend on payment or an available affiliate program. Do not add tracking or affiliate parameters without a real agreement, and disclose actual relationships beside the placement. Keep paid placements in the dedicated sponsorship/product slots described in [the publishing guide](docs/PUBLISHING.md).
